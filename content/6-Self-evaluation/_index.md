## Evaluation & Recommendations for the First Cloud AI Journey (FCJ) Program

These are my personal reflections after participating in the **AWS First Cloud Journey (FCJ)** internship program. The purpose of this page is to document my practical experience and offer a few suggestions to help improve the program for future cohorts of interns.

### Overall Evaluation

#### 1. Learning & Working Environment
The learning atmosphere in the group was quite open and pleasant. Everyone in the FCJ community, from mentors to peers, was enthusiastic about discussing things whenever I ran into issues, even outside of official study hours in the main group chat. Since the program is primarily online/self-paced, I had a lot of flexibility with my time; however, I sometimes felt a lack of direct connection. I think having more regular offline/team-building sessions among student groups in Ho Chi Minh City would help everyone bond and learn from each other better.

#### 2. Support from Mentors and Organizers
The mentors provided very detailed guidance, willingly explaining things multiple times when I didn't fully grasp an AWS concept or service, and always encouraged asking questions rather than holding back. What I appreciated most was that mentors didn't just give the answers right away; they usually suggested a direction for me to research and solve the problem myself, which significantly improved my self-learning ability. The program operations team also provided excellent support regarding materials, AWS credits, and answering questions related to study progress.

#### 3. Alignment Between Program Content and Academic Major
The program content aligned closely with what I learned at university (computer networking, basic programming) while expanding into many new areas that aren't taught in depth at school, such as cloud architecture, DevOps, and big data services on AWS. Because of this, I was able to both solidify my foundational knowledge and acquire practical skills to prepare for my future career.

#### 4. Opportunities for Learning and Skill Development
During my participation, besides technical knowledge about AWS, I also learned many soft skills such as self-study time management, teamwork through workshop sessions, and how to present/report progress more professionally. Having to read English documentation independently and do hands-on labs also helped me hone my independent research skills – a skill I believe will be very useful when entering the workforce.

#### 5. Community Culture & Spirit
The FCJ community brings a very positive vibe: people support each other regardless of whether they are new or long-term members, and questions in the group are usually answered quite quickly. During peak periods like preparing for assignments or the final project, I could feel a spirit of mutual effort, rather than everyone just looking out for themselves. This made me feel more connected to the program even though I was only participating as an intern.

#### 6. Support Policies for Interns
The program sponsored some AWS credits and limited accounts for doing labs, which helped reduce the financial pressure of learning. The flexible study schedule without mandatory fixed hours was also a huge plus for students still attending classes at university. In addition, the online sessions and offline workshops featuring AWS partners provided great opportunities to expand industry networks.

### Other Questions

**What were you most satisfied with during your internship?**
I was most satisfied with getting hands-on practice on real AWS accounts rather than just learning pure theory, along with the enthusiastic support from mentors whenever I faced difficulties during the labs.

**What do you think the program needs to improve for future interns?**
I think the program could add regular individual progress reviews (1-on-1 with a mentor) so students can adjust in time if they fall behind, as well as organize more offline networking sessions between student groups from different universities.

**If you were to recommend this to friends, would you advise them to join this program? Why?**
Yes, I would recommend it to my friends because the program is free, has a clear week-by-week roadmap, offers hands-on practice on AWS, and provides job referral opportunities upon completion – these are values that are hard to find in other courses.

### Suggestions & Wishes

**Suggestions to improve the internship experience:**
- Add a visual checklist/personal progress tracker so students can monitor which week they are on compared to the overall roadmap.
- Organize more mock interviews or 1-on-1 CV reviews before the program ends.
- Create separate Q&A channels by topic (Networking, Security, Database, etc.) for easier reference instead of mixing everything in one general chat group.

**Do you want to continue being involved with this program/community in the future?**
Yes. I want to continue participating as a content contributor (writing blogs/workshops to share knowledge) and aim for the opportunity to become an AWS Community Builder in the future, aligning with the program's shared orientation.

**Other Feedback:**
Overall, I highly rate the program's "learning by doing" spirit. If the program can maintain and expand the mentor network as well as increase the frequency of offline sessions, it will attract and retain even more students passionate about cloud technology.
